📘 Reselling tips

Before you buy

  • Check what it actually sells for. On eBay, search the card and tick Sold items. Asking prices run high; sold prices are the truth. If you can't sell it for clearly more than you pay plus fees, skip it.
  • Assume it's fake until proven otherwise. Compare every photo with the official card: font, colours, holo pattern, border width, energy symbols, the back. Gold/metal cards, blurry text and stock photos are red flags.
  • Ask for more photos (front, back, close-ups of corners and surface) from a seller with real feedback. No good photos, no buy.
  • Pay only through eBay so you're covered by the eBay Money Back Guarantee. Never pay by bank transfer or PayPal "friends and family" for a listing.
  • Start small. Set a monthly budget, buy cards you understand (Charizard, Pikachu, eeveelutions sell quickly) and learn from the first few before spending more.

Condition is everything

  • Check corners, edges, surface scratches and whitening on the back. Sell honestly as NM / LP / MP / HP. Over-grading leads to returns and bad feedback.
  • Near Mint, high-value cards (roughly £100+) can be worth grading (PSA, CGC or ACE in the UK). Grading costs money and takes weeks, so only do it when the graded price clearly covers it.

Selling

  • Where: eBay (biggest audience), Vinted (no seller fees, popular in the UK), Facebook groups and local card shops (quick, but they pay less).
  • Price just under the cheapest comparable sold price for a quick sale, or list higher with Best Offer turned on. Fast turnover beats waiting for a top price.
  • Great photos (both sides, good light, plain background) and an exact title: card name, set, number, condition, "English".

Posting

  • Card in a penny sleeve, then a toploader, inside a card-backed envelope. Use tracked postage for anything over about £20, and signed-for for valuable cards. Keep proof of postage.

Money and tax

  • Record every buy and sale (price, fees, postage) in a simple spreadsheet so you know your real profit.
  • Buying to resell for profit counts as trading. In the UK the first £1,000 a year of trading income is covered by the trading allowance; above that you need to register for Self Assessment. Selling platforms report sellers with 30+ sales or about £1,700+ a year to HMRC. Check gov.uk for current rules.
